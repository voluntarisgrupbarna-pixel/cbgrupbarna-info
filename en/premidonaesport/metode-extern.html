Academic framework

An academic study of grassroots basketball.

This document is the full version of the Barna Method as a study model: the ongoing academic research (UB-INEFC, 2026) that articulates five original theories on the hidden patterns of the gender gap in basketball, validated with 60 years of empirical evidence at CB Grup Barna.

It is presented this way because the Method is not a brand but a research framework open to replication, critique and evolution.

The neuroscientific pattern

Why the Method works at the brain level.

During puberty (ages 13-16), the brain undergoes a massive reorganization of the reward system (Steinberg, 2008). This creates a window of vulnerability in which adolescent girls abandon long-term commitments for instant gratification.

The Method addresses this without needing specific programmes:

  • M1
    The external scaffolding: stable routines substitute the internal discipline the teenager doesn't yet have.
  • M2
    The proximal role model: young coaches (20-25 years old) activate social learning mechanisms that a distant role model does not (Bandura, 1977).
  • M3
    Dopamine through sport: sport competes with screens in the reward circuit, but generates real neuroplasticity (Basso & Suzuki, 2016).

"Your daughter will study better if she plays" is an evidence-based argument that transforms family perception (Singh et al., 2018; Bidzan-Bluma & Lipowska, 2018).
