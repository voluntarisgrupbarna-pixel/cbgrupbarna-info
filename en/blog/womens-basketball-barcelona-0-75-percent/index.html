The 0.75% paradox: women's basketball in Barcelona

Barcelona has 1.6 million residents, 134 grassroots women's teams and one of Europe's largest development bases. And a 0.75% conversion rate to national categories. La Seu d'Urgell, with 12,000 residents, has 12.5%.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

The paradox, in one number

Of every 100 players trained in Barcelona, fewer than one goes on to compete at national level. 99.25% of the female talent the city develops disappears from the system. It isn't lost at one specific stage: it simply evaporates.

The first surprise is that size works against the city. The bigger the city, the worse the conversion:

  • La Seu d'Urgell — 12,000 residents, ~8 grassroots teams, 1 team in LF Endesa: 12.5%.
  • Girona — 103,000 residents, ~30 teams, 2 national-level teams: 6.7%.
  • Mataró — 130,000 residents, ~25 teams, 2 teams in LF2: 4%.
  • Madrid — 3.5 million, 100+ teams, 21 national-level teams: ~4%.
  • Barcelona — 1.6 million, 134 teams, 1 fragile team in LF Challenge: 0.75%.

Why it happens

There's no single culprit. There are five causes that feed off each other.

Facility saturation. In Barcelona the gym is the scarce resource. When hours run short, senior women's teams end up in the 9:30pm slots, and court rental can reach €110,000 a year for a club. The bill always lands on the same side.

The missing LF2 rung. A player who grows up in the city and has outgrown the regional level has nowhere to go without leaving. The ladder has a broken step right in the middle.

Talent drain. A consequence of the above: Cornellà, Mataró and Girona take in players trained in Barcelona. The city develops talent it can't keep.

Male-dominated governance. Across the 17 clubs analysed, 100% of chairs are men. Decisions about schedules, budgets and priorities are made from a single perspective.

Shadow effect. The presence of one global club shapes the whole ecosystem, and partnerships with the city's women's clubs haven't quite taken hold.

What the paradox doesn't explain

It's worth saying plainly: this isn't a problem of talent, vocation, or girls who don't want to play. With 134 grassroots teams, the raw material is all there. It's a problem of architecture: the system is designed in a way that loses, along the way, what it produces itself.

What can be done

The levers are known and none of them is impossible: bring back an LF2 tier in the city, distribute court time slots on equity criteria, specifically fund clubs with women's national representation or the potential for it, stabilise a calendar of women's events, and support technical training for the coaches already coaching.

The club has taken this diagnosis to Barcelona City Council's Sports Department (February 2026) and to the Catalan Basketball Federation (March 2026), with a 2026-2030 action framework for the city.

A case that proves it's possible

CB Grup Barna is today the #1 women's basketball club in Barcelona city by licences — 250 women's licences, a 6.61% share among 55 federated clubs — and ranks in Catalonia's TOP 12 out of 411 clubs. Put another way: one in every fifteen female basketball players in Barcelona trains in El Clot.

Without being a big or wealthy club. With a budget of €140,000-160,000, half of it earmarked for women's basketball. The uncomfortable conclusion for the sector is that the 50% isn't calling for more money: it's calling for distributing what already exists differently. The whole model is explained on the club's women's basketball page.

Frequently asked questions

What is the conversion rate for women's basketball in Barcelona?

0.75%. Of the players trained in the city, fewer than one in a hundred goes on to compete at national level, far below Girona (6.7%), Mataró (4%) or La Seu d'Urgell (12.5%).

How many women's basketball teams are there in Barcelona?

Around 134 grassroots women's teams, spread across 55 federated clubs in the city.

Why do Barcelona players have to move to other cities?

Because Barcelona lacks the LF2 tier. Once a player outgrows the regional level, there's nowhere to continue in the city and she has to go to Cornellà, Mataró or Girona.

Which club has the most women's basketball players in Barcelona?

CB Grup Barna, with 250 women's licences and a 6.61% share of the city: one in every fifteen female basketball players in Barcelona trains there.

Does improving women's basketball require a bigger budget?

Not necessarily. CB Grup Barna runs on €140,000-160,000 a year and allocates 50% to women's basketball, when the sector norm is around 35%. The difference is in the distribution, not the volume.
