Women's basketball

Basketball coaches: why it matters who's on the bench

35.7% of basketball licenses in Spain belong to women. Among Barcelona club presidencies, 0%. Between one extreme and the other there's a ladder that loses people at every rung — always the same people.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

The ladder of recognition

The gender gap in sport isn't well measured by counting how many play. It's measured by looking at how many move from one rung to the next. These are basketball's numbers:

  • Licensed players: 35.7%. A healthy base.
  • Coaches in training (levels 0-3): 24.1%. A third are lost along the way.
  • Advanced Coaching Course: 9%. Here, almost two out of every three drop out.
  • Territorial federation presidencies: 5%. Token.
  • Barcelona club presidencies: 0%. None, across the 17 clubs analysed.

When the drop between two consecutive rungs exceeds 40%, you're no longer looking at a matter of vocation: you're looking at a barrier. And there are three of them in a row.

An empty bench isn't neutral

A girl who spends ten years at a club without ever seeing a woman lead a team learns something no one explicitly taught her. No one needs to tell her that coaching isn't for her — it's enough that she never sees one.

There's a detail in this story that's hard to swallow and matters: when players first see a female coach, they tend to rate her as less competent than a male coach. But sustained exposure reverses the bias completely — players who have had women coaches rate them as equally competent as men. That means the remedy isn't a talk: it's normality repeated for years.

The moment everything changes: a woman coaching a boys' team

Within a club, the truly transformative step isn't a woman coaching the girls' team. It's her coaching the boys' team. When that happens, three things occur at once that no equality programme can manufacture:

  • Girls see that leading is a normal role for a woman.
  • Boys grow up with a female sporting authority figure, and it seems perfectly normal to them because it's all they've ever known.
  • Families see for themselves that a woman is competent to coach their son. That moves more than any campaign.

The qualification paradox

CB Grup Barna has 38 active coaches. They make up 65.5% of the technical staff and coach 40 teams every week. None of them holds a federation card — not for lack of ability, but because of the course's cost and the schedule it demands, which clashes with studying or working.

This has a consequence that goes far beyond the club: for the official statistics, these 38 women don't exist. Multiply that by every club in Catalonia in the same situation and you get figures for women in technical roles that fall well short of reality. And sports policy is designed around those figures.

On top of that, support programmes usually require a qualification to access them. In other words: you need to already have what the programme is supposed to help you get.

The Barna 8M Fund

On 4 March 2026 the club formally submitted a proposal to the Catalan Basketball Federation to break this cycle: a scholarship fund to cover the Level 0 Sports Coach course, co-financed by the club and the federation, with a two-season commitment to stay. The club has 38 immediate candidates for it.

The second part of the proposal costs no money: changing the access criteria for the mentoring programme from "holding a prior qualification" to "being an active coach at a federated club." An entry route based on real activity, not paperwork.

The full formal request, with the letter to the FCBQ president and the proposal's three levels, is available at Barna 8M Fund.

Why this should matter to a family

Because the number of coaches a club has is one of the most honest indicators there is. It can't be improvised for a visit or dressed up in a brochure: either they're there or they're not, and you have them in front of you every Tuesday. If you're club-hunting, count them.

Frequently asked questions

How many coaches does CB Grup Barna have?

38 active coaches, representing 65.5% of the club's technical staff, who coach 40 teams every week.

What percentage of basketball coaches are women overall?

Women make up 35.7% of player licenses, 24.1% of staff in technical training, and only 9% of the Advanced Coaching Course. Among the Barcelona clubs analysed, 0% of presidencies.

Do you need a qualification to coach at a grassroots club?

The federation qualification is the formal route, but many active coaches don't have it because of the course's cost and schedule. The Barna 8M Fund proposes funding it in exchange for a commitment to stay at the club.

What is the Barna 8M Fund?

A proposal from CB Grup Barna submitted to the Catalan Basketball Federation on 4 March 2026 to fund qualifications for active coaches and change the access criteria for mentoring programmes.

Why does it matter when a woman coaches a boys' team?

Because it normalises female sporting authority for girls, boys and families all at once. It's sustained exposure, not messaging, that changes perception.
