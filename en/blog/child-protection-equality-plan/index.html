Child protection and Equality Plan: what the club has in writing

A basketball club is, above all, a place where children spend many hours a week. That means having things in writing, approved, with a named person responsible. These are CB Grup Barna's, explained for families.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

Why we're publishing this

These documents usually live in a folder no one opens. We're putting them here for two reasons. First, because a family has the right to know what happens if a problem ever arises, before the problem exists. Second, because what isn't published can't be demanded: if it's written down and public, you can hold us to it.

These are three separate documents, and it's useful not to mix them up: the Equality Plan sets out how we distribute resources and opportunities; the anti-harassment protocol says what happens if someone crosses a line; and child protection is the legal framework protecting the club's children.

Child protection · LOPIVI

The club complies with Organic Law 8/2021 on the comprehensive protection of children and adolescents against violence, known as the LOPIVI. In practice, that means four concrete things:

  • The club's own protection protocol, not a generic template downloaded from the internet.
  • A Child Protection Officer with specific training. This is the role the law requires of organisations working with children, and at Barna it has a name and a face.
  • A clean sexual offence certificate held by every member of staff: coaches, delegates, volunteers and the board.
  • A confidential communication channel so families, players and staff can report any risky situation, or simply a concern, without needing to be certain of anything.

This last point is the hardest to explain and the most important: you don't need to be sure of anything to raise a flag. The channel exists precisely for doubts.

2026-2029 Equality Plan

Approved by the Board of Directors in April 2026, valid for three years with an annual review. It applies to everyone connected to the club: players, coaching staff, volunteers, families, the board and external collaborators. It has five action lines.

Line 1 · Equal investment

Keeping 50% of the total budget in women's basketball, guaranteeing equal access to facilities, equipment and medical services, and publishing the budget breakdown by gender every year. That third commitment is what makes the other two verifiable.

Line 2 · Female leadership pipeline

Keeping the full women's pathway — escoleta, development, competition, coaching, senior — and driving the Barna 8M Fund to finance coach certification. Target: reaching 70% female technical staff within three years.

Line 3 · Equal governance

A minimum of 40% women on the board of directors, progressively bringing women into coordination roles, team delegate positions and committees, and involving mothers and former players in decision-making bodies. This is the line where the club has the most ground to cover, which is why there's a number attached.

Line 4 · Non-stereotyped communication

Equal visibility for women's and men's teams on social media and the website, without segregating sports into "girls'" and "boys'" categories, and highlighting female athletes as role models for girls and boys alike.

Line 5 · Training and awareness

A gender perspective in themed camps, raising family awareness of women's sport as a health issue, and collaborating with universities (UB and INEFC) on gender and sport research.

The indicators, with numbers

A plan without indicators is a statement. Here are the club's, with today's value and the 2028 target:

  • Ratio of men's to women's teams: 1:1 today → maintain.
  • Female technical staff: 65.5% → 70%.
  • Investment in women's basketball: 50% → maintain.
  • Women on the board of directors: 2 → at least 40%.
  • Coaches with a federation licence: 0 of 38 → 10 of 38.
  • Female audience on social media: 53.7% → keep above 50%.

The licence figure is the starkest and most honest one: today none of the club's 38 female coaches holds a federation licence, not for lack of ability but because of the course's cost and schedule. We cover this in full in the article on coaches and the ladder of recognition.

Protocol against sexual and gender-based harassment

Also approved in April 2026, under the framework of Organic Law 3/2007 on effective equality and Law 17/2020 on women's right to eradicate gender-based violence. It applies inside and outside the facility, and covers players of every category, coaching staff, volunteers, the board, families and referees.

What counts as harassment

Any verbal, non-verbal or physical behaviour of a sexual nature that violates a person's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading or offensive environment. And also gender-based harassment: sexist comments, discrimination based on pregnancy or motherhood, and any unfavourable treatment based on gender.

Prevention

The protocol is circulated at the start of every season, basic equality training is given to the whole coaching staff, a commitment clause appears on registration forms, posters with the communication channel are displayed around the club, and a designated point person is in place.

What happens when someone reports something

  1. Reporting. Anyone who experiences it, witnesses it or becomes aware of it can report it to the point person, in person, by phone or by email. With absolute confidentiality.
  2. Initial assessment within 72 hours. The point person listens to those affected and gathers information. If minors are involved, their legal guardians are informed immediately.
  3. Precautionary measures. If there are signs of risk, the board can immediately suspend the reported person from activity, before any resolution is reached.
  4. Resolution within a maximum of 15 days. Sanctions can range from a warning to permanent expulsion. If the facts may constitute a crime, the club cooperates with the authorities.

Three guarantees

There's no retaliation against anyone who reports something. The confidentiality of everyone involved is preserved throughout the process. And anyone affected has the right to support and emotional accompaniment for as long as it lasts.

How it's activated, in practice

The equality point person and the Child Protection Officer are introduced to every family at the start of each season, with contact details. If you need to know who they are or how to reach them right now, write to the club and we'll tell you the same day: the club's WhatsApp or info@cbgrupbarna.com.

You don't need to explain anything or have proof to ask.

Review and transparency

Both documents are reviewed annually. The Equality Plan's indicators are published in the club's annual report, along with the budget breakdown by gender. If you're interested in the broader framework behind these documents, it's on the women's basketball page and on the website for the Women and Sport Award nomination.

And if you're comparing clubs, we have a list of eleven questions to ask any organisation before signing anyone up. They work for us and for everyone else.

Frequently asked questions

Does CB Grup Barna have an equality plan?

Yes. The 2026-2029 Equality Plan, approved by the Board of Directors in April 2026, with five action lines, tracking indicators with 2028 targets and an annual review.

What does it mean for a club to comply with the LOPIVI?

That it has its own child protection protocol, a designated child protection officer with specific training, a confidential communication channel, and a clean sexual offence certificate held by every staff member.

Do all the coaches have a sexual offence certificate?

Yes. Every member of the club's staff — coaching staff, delegates, volunteers and the board of directors — holds a clean sexual offence certificate, as required by law for anyone working with minors.

How is a harassment situation reported to the club?

To the equality point person, in person, by phone or electronically. The protocol guarantees confidentiality, an initial assessment within a maximum of 72 hours, and resolution within a maximum of 15 days.

Is there retaliation for reporting something?

No. The protocol expressly guarantees no retaliation against anyone who reports a situation, confidentiality for everyone involved, and the right to support and emotional accompaniment throughout the process.

What equality targets does the club have for 2028?

Going from 65.5% to 70% female technical staff, reaching at least 40% women on the board of directors, getting 10 of the 38 female coaches licensed, and maintaining the 1:1 team balance and 50% investment in women's basketball.
