League competition or not: the first decision

This is the question that avoids disappointment, the same one covered in school, club or basketball academy?. A beginner school works on fundamentals with no competition: it is for learning to play and having fun, and that is fine if that is what you want. A club with league teams competes in the FCBQ from the youngest age groups: a child who starts there can stay for their whole playing career. The problem comes when a family wants a competitive pathway and picks a school that has none, or the other way round.

The Barna Escoleta has both inside the same club: it starts with no competition at age 4 and, for those who want to continue, carries on into the FCBQ league youth basketball pathway without changing club or court.

The four criteria that decide

1. League competition or not. Decides whether there is a real sporting pathway beyond the beginner stage.

2. Coach-to-player ratio. The smaller the group, the more individual attention and ball repetitions per child.

3. Distance from home. A school ten minutes' walk away lasts for years; one half an hour's drive away often does not make it past the second season.

4. Whether there is a genuine girls' pathway. "Mixed team" is not enough: check whether there is the same number of teams and the same budget for girls and boys across every age group, not just the beginner one.

By age

Most schools in Barcelona take children from age 4-5, with a focus on motor skills and first contact with the ball. League competition for youth teams usually starts around age 8. At Barna, the Escoleta runs from age 4 to 8 and the youth basketball pathway covers every age group up to cadet and junior, always in groups by age and level.

The CB Grup Barna Escoleta, in one line

A basketball school in El Clot with a real pathway through to league competition: mixed groups by age from age 4, qualified coaches and the same sporting ceiling for boys and girls in every age group. It is run by Julio Torralba, who founded the club in 1965 and, at 72, is still an active Escoleta coach every week. The first session is a trial with no commitment. This same court has produced Javier Torralba (coach of Valencia Basket's women's team), Ainhoa López (captain of Spar Girona), Roger Fornas (ACB and LEB Oro) and David Mejía (Tercera FEB champion).

Frequently asked questions

What is the best basketball school in Barcelona?

There is no single best school. For brand and the big-club experience, Barça Escola; for playful beginner sessions with no competition, schools such as Nacho Solozábal's; for a neighbourhood school with a real pathway to league competition, with the same ceiling for boys and girls, the CB Grup Barna Escoleta in El Clot. What decides it is whether there is competition, the ratio, the distance from home and whether there is a genuine girls' pathway.

What is the difference from a club with league teams?

A beginner school works on fundamentals with no competition; a club with league teams competes in the FCBQ from a young age. At Barna, the Escoleta and the youth basketball pathway coexist inside the same club: a child can start with no competition and stay for their whole playing career without changing clubs.

Do you need prior experience to sign up?

No. At the Barna Escoleta the first session is a trial with no commitment, and groups are formed by age, not by prior level.

Is there a basketball school for girls?

Yes. The Escoleta is mixed and the club has real parity between men's and women's basketball, with girls' teams in every age group.
