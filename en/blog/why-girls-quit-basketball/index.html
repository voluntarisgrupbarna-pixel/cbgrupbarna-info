Why girls quit basketball in adolescence

At 15 and 16, only 8% of girls meet the recommended minimum levels of physical activity. Boys reach it at 23.2%. The comfortable reading is that they quit. The uncomfortable one is that the system pushes them out.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

The figure, and the trap in how we read it

The 2024-25 Sporting Habits Survey says that only 8% of girls aged 15 and 16 meet the minimum levels of physical activity, against 23.2% of boys. Almost three times fewer.

The usual way of explaining it puts the verb on them: they «quit», they «lose interest», «it gets complicated with school». When you look at what lies behind each individual departure, though, the verb changes sides. It is not that they leave: it is that a moment arrives when carrying on becomes expensive, uncomfortable or simply impossible. And that does not happen by chance, it happens because of how clubs are designed.

The five mechanisms that push players out

At the club we have identified them and given them names, because we have seen them at work at other organisations and, at times, at home too. None of them is bad faith: they are organisational decisions nobody took with the aim of pushing anyone out.

  • 1. There is no next rung. Many clubs have girls' teams at under-12 and under-14, but not at under-18 or senior level. When the under-16 player looks for somewhere to carry on, her club has no place for her. She does not quit: she has nowhere to go.
  • 2. The hostile timetable. Senior women's teams tend to be pushed into the 9.30pm to 11pm slots, the ones nobody else wants. A 17-year-old cannot train at ten at night on a school Tuesday. The one who quits has chosen nothing.
  • 3. The empty bench. If a ten-year-old girl has never seen a woman coaching, she receives a message nobody has said out loud: leading a team is a man's job. Absence speaks too.
  • 4. The forced exodus. Players with potential have to move to Cornellà, Mataró or Girona to find the level that does not exist in Barcelona. At 16, that means choosing between basketball and the life you have.
  • 5. The access paradox. Support programmes for women coaches tend to require a prior qualification in order to help you obtain the qualification. A circular design that excludes exactly the people it meant to include.

Why precisely at puberty

Because that is when everything happens at once. Between 13 and 16 the brain reorganises the reward system and a window of vulnerability opens in which stable routines are worth their weight in gold. Organised sport works, at this stage, as external scaffolding: fixed days, people expecting you, something you are good at.

It is also the age at which many families decide to drop the sport «to focus on school». The research points the other way: exercise improves concentration, memory and school performance. Taking her out of the team in Year 8 usually costs on both counts.

And there is something else that is rarely discussed: the clothing. Studies on teenage dropout find, time and again, that constant worry about physical appearance because of the uniform appears among the reasons for quitting. We cover it separately in the article about kit.

What a club should do

Nothing spectacular, and all of it at once. Have a team in every girls' age group so that nobody ever has to leave. Allocate court slots on an equity basis and not by seniority. Have women coaches visible from day one. And not run campaigns: just do it.

It is worth asking before signing anyone up anywhere. Are there under-18 and senior women's teams? What time does the senior women's team train? How many women coaches are there? The answers say more than any statement of intent.

How we do it in the Clot

At CB Grup Barna there are girls' teams in every age group, from the Escoleta at 4 years old to the senior team, so that no player has to fend for herself when she moves up an age group. The girls' and women's teams get priority access to the mid-afternoon slots. And there are 38 active women coaches, 65.5% of the technical staff, coaching girls', mixed and boys' teams.

The club devotes 50% of its budget to it when the sector norm is around 35%. Not because it is an equality policy, but because that is how it has been built since 1965.

Frequently asked questions

At what age do most girls quit sport?

The critical point falls between 13 and 16. At 15-16 only 8% of girls meet the recommended minimum levels of physical activity, against 23.2% of boys.

Is it true that quitting sport improves school results?

The research points the other way: regular physical exercise improves concentration, memory and school performance. Taking a teenage girl out of sport so she can study more is usually counterproductive.

What should I ask a club to know whether it will keep my daughter?

Three questions say almost everything: whether there are girls' teams at under-18 and senior level, what time the senior women's team trains, and how many women coaches the club has.

Why does it matter that there are women coaches?

Because a girl who never sees a woman on the bench receives the implicit message that leading a team is not for her. Sustained exposure to women coaches reverses that bias, in girls, in boys and in families alike.

Does CB Grup Barna have girls' teams in every age group?

Yes. From the Escoleta for ages 4 to 8 all the way to the senior team, taking in under-10, under-12, under-14, under-16 and under-18. In the 2026-27 season there are 8 girls' and women's teams in FCBQ federated competition.
