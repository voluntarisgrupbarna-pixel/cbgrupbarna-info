Women basketball coaches: why who is on the bench matters

Women hold 35.7% of basketball licences in Spain. Among Barcelona's club presidencies, 0%. Between one end and the other there is a ladder that loses people at every rung, and always the same people.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

The ladder of recognition

The gender gap in sport is not properly measured by counting how many women play. It is measured by looking at how many move from one rung to the next. These are basketball's numbers:

  • Licensed players: 35.7%. A healthy base.
  • Coaches in training (levels 0-3): 24.1%. A third is lost along the way.
  • Advanced Coaching Course: 9%. Here almost two out of every three leave.
  • Regional federation presidencies: 5%. Token.
  • Barcelona club presidencies: 0%. Across the 17 clubs analysed, none.

When the drop between two consecutive rungs is more than 40%, you are no longer looking at a question of commitment: you are looking at a barrier. And there are three of them in a row.

An empty bench is not neutral

A girl who spends ten years at a club without ever seeing a woman running a team learns something nobody has taught her explicitly. She does not need to be told coaching is not for her: it is enough that she never sees a woman do it.

There is a detail in this story that is hard to swallow and that matters: when players see a woman coach for the first time, they tend to rate her as less competent than a male coach. But sustained exposure reverses the bias completely — players who have had women coaches rate them exactly as competent as men. Which means the remedy is not a talk: it is normality repeated over years.

The moment everything changes: a woman coaching a boys' team

Inside a club, the genuinely transformative step is not a woman coaching the girls' team. It is her coaching the boys' team. When that happens, three things occur at once that no equality programme can manufacture:

  • Girls see that leading a team is a normal role for a woman.
  • Boys grow up with a female figure of sporting authority, and it strikes them as entirely normal because it is all they have known.
  • Families see for themselves that a woman is competent to coach their son. That shifts more than any campaign.

The qualification paradox

CB Grup Barna has 38 active women coaches. They are 65.5% of the technical staff and they coach 34+ teams every week. None holds a federation licence — not for lack of ability, but because of the cost of the course and the timetable it demands, which clashes with studying or working.

That has a consequence reaching far beyond the club: as far as official statistics are concerned, these 38 women do not exist. Multiply that by every club in Catalonia in the same position and you get figures for women in technical roles far below reality. And sports policy is designed using those figures.

On top of that, support programmes tend to require a qualification to join. That is: you need to have the very thing the programme should be helping you obtain.

Fons Barna 8M

On 4 March 2026 the club formally put to the Catalan Basketball Federation a proposal to break this circle: a grant fund covering the Level 0 Sports Coach course with co-funding between the club and the federation, in return for a two-season commitment. The club has 38 immediate candidates.

The second part of the proposal costs nothing: change the entry criterion for the mentoring programme, from «holding a prior qualification» to «being an active coach at a federated club». A route in based on what you actually do, not on accreditation.

The full formal request, with the letter to the FCBQ president and the three levels of the proposal, is at Fons Barna 8M.

Why this should matter to a family

Because the number of women coaches at a club is one of the most honest indicators there is. It cannot be improvised for a visit or dressed up in a brochure: either they are there or they are not, and you see them every Tuesday. If you are looking for a club, count them.

Frequently asked questions

How many women coaches does CB Grup Barna have?

38 active women coaches, who make up 65.5% of the club's technical staff and coach 34+ teams every week.

What percentage of coaches in basketball as a whole are women?

Women hold 35.7% of player licences, 24.1% of places in technical training and only 9% on the Advanced Coaching Course. Among the presidencies of the Barcelona clubs analysed, 0%.

Do you need a qualification to coach at a youth club?

The federation qualification is the formal route, but many active coaches do not hold it because of the cost and the timetable of the course. Fons Barna 8M proposes funding it in return for a commitment to stay at the club.

What is Fons Barna 8M?

A CB Grup Barna proposal put to the Catalan Basketball Federation on 4 March 2026 to fund qualifications for active women coaches and change the entry criteria for mentoring programmes.

Why does it matter that a woman coaches a boys' team?

Because it normalises female sporting authority for girls, boys and families all at once. It is sustained exposure, not communications, that changes perception.
