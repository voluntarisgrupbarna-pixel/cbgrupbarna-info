The Barna Method: 60 years of women's basketball in El Clot

The long version of how Barna's women's basketball went from being "the other team" to being half the club — with data, names, and the full comparison against the clubs considered the big names of Catalonia.

Also available in: català · castellano

Where the Barna Method comes from

For decades, in grassroots basketball —at Barna and at most clubs in Catalonia— the women's section grew as an extension of the men's: fewer teams, fewer hours on good courts, less budget. The turning point at Barna was turning that observation into a project with its own name and its own research: the Barna Method, a model for women's skills development built on an internal study of the gender gap in basketball in Barcelona and Catalonia —how many girls start, how many stay, at what point they drop out, and why.

The result isn't a manifesto, it's concrete decisions: the same budget for both lines, the same double-team design (A and B) at every women's category as at the men's, and coaches trained specifically to support the moment when, historically, more girls used to leave the sport.

The recognition: Premi Dona i Esport

The Barna Method has been a finalist for the Premi Dona i Esport in two consecutive editions, reaching the 19th edition of the award in 2026. It's external recognition, not self-awarded, that the model works beyond El Clot.

Where Barna stands among the big clubs

The comparison with FC Barcelona, la Penya, Bàsquet Manresa and Fundació Bàsquet Girona —who has their own senior team, in which league and since when— is a matter of positioning for the whole club, not just the women's project, and has its own section.

Names: where Barna's players have gone

Ainhoa López started at the Escoleta of CB Grup Barna and today is captain of Spar Girona in the Liga Femenina Endesa, the top national category. She's proof that the complete pathway —Escoleta, premini, mini, infantil, cadet, júnior, senior— can lead all the way to the top without ever leaving grassroots basketball.

The same court has seen Javier Torralba go on to become coach of Valencia Basket Femení in the same league — technical training, not just sporting.

Who makes it possible

The club's founder, Julio Torralba, still coaches the Escoleta every week at 72 years old. Sixty years after founding Barna, he's still the one who welcomes the first girls and boys who step onto a basketball court.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between Barna and Barça in women's basketball?

FC Barcelona has had no women's basketball team since its project (Barça CBS, in collaboration with CB Santfeliuenc) closed at the end of the 2024-25 season for financial reasons. Barna, by contrast, has women's basketball at every category, from the Escoleta to Senior A in the Super Copa Femenina FCBQ.

What makes the Barna Method different from other clubs?

It's built on the club's own research into the gender gap in basketball in Barcelona and Catalonia, applied in concrete ways: the same budget, the same level of technical demand, and the same double-team design (A and B) as the men's line at every category.

Which players have come up through Barna's women's basketball?

Among others, Ainhoa López, today captain of Spar Girona in the Liga Femenina Endesa, started at the Escoleta of CB Grup Barna.
