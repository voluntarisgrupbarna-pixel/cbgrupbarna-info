How to Choose a Basketball Club in Barcelona

Almost every basketball club in Barcelona says the same thing on its website: "we develop people", "we're a family", "the best atmosphere". None of those phrases tell one club apart from another. These six criteria do.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-28

1. Whether it fields teams at every age group, or only the youngest ones

Many clubs are strong at school age (4-8) and thin out as children get older, until at 14-15 there is only one team left, or none at all. Asking how many teams the club has at each age group — not just "how many teams does it have" — tells you whether a child can grow up there all the way through, or will need to find a new club halfway.

2. Whether the girls' side is real, or a single team

"We have a girls' team" says nothing on its own. Ask how many girls' teams there are, at which age groups, and how far up they go. A club with girls only at school age and none at senior level is a club where girls end up leaving for lack of a team to move up to — a pattern well documented in Catalan basketball, not one worth repeating with your daughter.

3. The real level of competition

There's a big difference between a club that plays a school-district league and one that competes across every FCBQ (Catalan Basketball Federation) age group, at the corresponding levels, up to the Super Copa if it gets there. Neither option is "better" in the abstract: it depends on whether you're after neighbourhood-level competition or sporting ambition. What does matter is that the club states clearly what level each team plays at, rather than leaving it vague.

4. Child protection, in writing and with someone accountable

A basketball club is, above all, a place where children spend many hours a week, often without a parent present. That should mean concrete, checkable things: a child-protection protocol approved by the board (not a generic sentence), a clean criminal record certificate for everyone who works with minors, and a named, specifically trained person to call if something happens. If a club's website doesn't say this with that level of detail, it's worth asking before signing anyone up.

5. Distance from home

A club across town gets abandoned the first rainy winter. A neighbourhood club lasts for years, because the child can walk there alone as they grow up, and because their teammates end up being the same kids from school and the street.

6. Whether they let you try before committing

Any club that trusts what it does will let you come for a full session, free of charge, with nothing to decide yet. If they make that difficult — you must register first, you must pay a fee just to try — that already answers how much they trust their own offer.

Frequently asked questions

What criteria matter when choosing a basketball club in Barcelona?

Six, mainly: whether it fields teams at every age group or only the youngest ones, whether the girls' side is real, the level of competition, whether it has a written child-protection protocol, the distance from home, and whether they let you try before committing.

Is a big club better than a neighbourhood club?

It depends what you're looking for — there's no single answer. A big club usually offers a higher competitive level and more resources; a neighbourhood club usually offers proximity, continuity and a closer relationship. What matters is that, big or small, it has full structure across every age group and the same care for both genders.

Does a basketball club need to have a real girls' side?

Yes, if there's a daughter who wants to play. Look past whether they "have a girls' team": how many girls' teams there are, at which age groups, and whether they reach senior level. A club with girls only at school age and no senior team is one where they're likely to end up leaving.

Can you try a basketball club before signing up?

At any serious club, yes, and free of charge. At CB Grup Barna, for instance, all of September is open days; any child can come for a free, no-commitment trial session.

Where does CB Grup Barna stand in this comparison?

It's a club with teams at every age group from age 4, a real girls' side (8 federated teams, 50% of the budget, 65.5% of the coaching staff), a written child-protection protocol and open days all through September. The full comparison with Barcelona's other clubs is on the club's positioning page.
