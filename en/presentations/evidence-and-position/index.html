Executive summary

Six conclusions that hold up the whole document

  1. Development first is not ideology: it is what the evidence says. Enjoyment predicts staying in sport (r = 0.45) almost three times better than competitive performance (r = 0.18), and 92.8% of those who stand out at junior level are not those who stand out as seniors. A club that retains players by winning is using the weakest lever there is.
  2. The model has a famous, documented precedent. Marc Gasol founded a school in 2014 —not a team— and did not create his first senior side until 2017. Thirteen verified public items, from 2014 to 2026, sustain his doctrine: «We start with the utmost humility, with commitment, effort and a methodology of our own» and «We are not going to rush to reach the ACB».
  3. The Barna is the third largest development structure in its comparable field. 32 teams federated with the FCBQ in 2026-27 (28 youth + 4 senior). Only Boet Mataró (42) and UE Mataró (37) register more — and of the three, the Barna is the only one without a team in the FEB categories.
  4. The leading development club in the city of Barcelona for digital community. 7,096 followers, 6th of 22 verified Catalan and Spanish development clubs. The five ahead are all from the metropolitan area, not from the city.
  5. Where others need two or three organisations, the Barna does it with one. Barça, Girona, Manresa, Sant Adrià, Cornellà and L'Hospitalet split men's, women's and youth basketball across separate legal entities and accounts. The Barna covers everything under a single brand: for a sponsor, a single point of entry to the whole community.
  6. The ceiling is not the structure, it is the conversion. 222 followers per federated team, against Sant Adrià's 532 or Almeda's 455. The structure is already built; what is missing is editorial identity.
How to read this report. Parts I to III answer why we develop players this way and where the model comes from. Parts IV to VI answer where we stand in comparable numbers. The methodological note at the end documents the discrepancies between sources and which one prevails in each case.
Part I

The executive summary, in full

Full reproduction of the Executive summary of the state of the art (Ana Fernández Durán, August 2026), the only document from the research attached in this session.

I·1 · What this document is

A doctoral-level review

A summary of a doctoral-level literature review on the youth basketball club: eleven parallel lines of research, more than 900 searches of academic search engines, institutional repositories, bibliographic APIs and primary federation, legal and government sources. The full report runs to ~61,000 words and eighteen chapters.

It covers the psychology of the young athlete, dropout, talent development, maturation and health, parents and coaches, child protection, the sociology of the club, international models, management and governance, sport theory, gender and feminism, and the economics of basketball clubs. It includes a map of Catalan basketball and a chapter applied to CB Grup Barna.

Its distinctive feature is traceability. Every reference carries a verification mark, and Annex III lists exhaustively what could not be verified — including five widely circulated figures in the sector that must not be cited.

I·2 · Findings

The fifteen findings that matter most

On dropout

Finding 1
12,93

Dropout concentrates at 12–14, not at 11

Real mean age: 12.93. It coincides exactly with the under-14 → under-16 transition in Spanish youth basketball.

Finding 2
r = 0.45

Enjoyment is the dominant predictor of persistence

Sporting competence is one of the weakest (r = 0.18). A club that retains players through performance is using the weakest lever there is.

Finding 3
86 vs 58%

What retains players is not pressure but presence

Basketball players report the highest levels of negative parental behaviour of any sport analysed. And what retains them: 86% parental attendance at games among current players against 58% among former players.

Finding 4
70 · 59 %

It is not a loss of interest: it is environmental expulsion

70% of Catalan girls quit on reaching adolescence, but 59% of those who quit still enjoy competitive sport.

On talent

Finding 5
92,8 %

Early identification does not work

92.8% of the junior and senior elite populations are different people.

Finding 6
50,3 / 6,8

Relative age selects but does not predict performance

In Spain it is extreme: 50.3% born in the first quarter and 6.8% in the fourth in the regional selections (n = 1,368).

Finding 7
U12–U15

Maturation predicts the physical, not the technical

Biological maturation predicts power, speed and agility, but not technical skill. Selecting on physique at U12–U15 is, to a large extent, selecting on biological date of birth.

On health and club culture

Finding 8
−36 %

Ten minutes of neuromuscular warm-up

2–3 times a week cut ankle and knee injuries in youth basketball by 36% — with an equivalent effect with and without external supervision.

Finding 9
Elite

The serious problems come from the best

The sport ethic of Hughes and Coakley explains harm through overconformity: the player who hides pain is not breaking the club's culture, he is embodying it. Burnout is a problem of the club's social organisation, not a personal failing of the player.

Finding 10
78,1 %

Violence and positive experience coexist

78.1% of the Spanish respondents in the European CASES project reported interpersonal violence within sport before the age of 18 (69.4% psychological) — and 85% simultaneously reported positive experiences. Both figures coexist.

On the economics

Finding 11
×5 · ×36

The cost ladder of promotion is discontinuous

×5 at the first step into the national tier, ×2–3 at each rung after that. The first one —the one a neighbourhood club takes— is the most brutal in relative terms. From Tercera to Primera FEB, the fee is multiplied by 36 and the bond by almost 19.

Finding 12
2 cases

The adjustment is made in the youth section and the women's section

Força Lleida, 2024: promotion of the men's team to the ACB was financed, among other things, by withdrawing the women's team from Liga Femenina 2. Betis / Baloncesto Sevilla, 2023: €2.6m cut by eliminating two youth teams and the under-12 and under-10 age groups — and even so, insolvency proceedings in 2026 and the club's disappearance.

Finding 13
0,61 %

The FEB devotes 0.61% of its budget to development

€193,762 against more than €8 million to elite competition.

On gender and management

Finding 14
90 → 40 %

Equal access does not produce equal power

Title IX multiplied the number of US high-school female players ninefold and cut women coaches from 90% to 40%. In Catalonia, male presidencies of sports clubs have been stuck at 88% for six years while women's licences grow.

Finding 15
~84 %

The dominant constraint is not money: it is the board

Three independent sources place it above funding as a perceived problem, and ~84% of the economic value of Catalan sports volunteering falls on the boards.

I·3 · The intervention

The best documented cost-impact ratio

A workshop of 75 minutes to 2 hours of behavioural training for coaches cut dropout from 26% to 5% in a design with a control group
Barnett, Smoll and Smith, 1992

Three independent lines converge with it: the coach is the dimension that most predicts retention in Spanish sports centres; the caring/transformational climate outperforms every other predictor of youth outcomes (r = 0.43, across 177 studies); and in the SPLISS model, the pillar that sets Serbia and Lithuania apart is not funding but coach education.

A necessary caveat: a four-month Spanish study cut punitive behaviour by more than half without improving the climate as perceived by the players, and two systematic reviews note that institutional maintenance is almost never documented. Training once is not enough: it has to be recurrent and the climate has to be measured as the players perceive it, not by coach attendance.

I·5 · The case

What can be stated before the Board, the District and sponsors

What can be stated

  • TEAM sport behaves in the opposite way to individual sport with regard to adolescent mental health: −10% in anxiety/depression, −19% in depressive symptoms with withdrawal and −20% in rule-breaking behaviour in girls, against +16% anxiety/depression in individual sport (n = 11,235). It is the most specific argument available to a basketball club.
  • The social value of an active child is almost double that of an active adult: £4,300/year (ages 11–16) against £2,600.
  • A volunteer generates more than twice the social value per head of a participant (£2,663 against £1,274).
  • The social return of sport has a methodologically published measure: SROI of 1.91 (2019), 3.28 (2020) and 4.38 (2025) per pound invested.
  • The fabric of clubs is inherited capital and is not reproducible. A club with sixty-one years of history in the Clot is not an interchangeable service provider.
  • In Sant Martí the income gap between neighbourhoods of the same district is almost 3:1 (Vila Olímpica +41% on the Barcelona average; el Besòs i el Maresme −63%). Fees, equipment and travel are not administrative details: they are the barrier.
  • Basketball is the Spanish team sport with the best gender trend: 36.1% women's licences in 2025, with female growth that in 2024 more than doubled the male figure.

What must not be stated

  • That taking part in sport causes integration, better educational performance or better mental health. The designs are mostly cross-sectional and the effects small (10–20%).
  • That sport improves grades. The effect on academic performance is mixed; the solid effect is on sustained attention behaviour in the classroom (d = 0.77) and on self-esteem. It is a better argument and it also happens to be true.

The defensible formulation is Coalter's: sport is a necessary but not sufficient condition. What produces the effects are the sustained social relationships and the complementary supports the club organises around the sport.

I·6 · No-go zone

Five figures that circulate and must NOT be cited

«70% of children quit before the age of 13»
No verifiable primary source. Use instead: a mean dropout age of 12.93 and 30% annual dropout from at least one club.
«Maturational variability of ±5 years»
Not verifiable. Use instead: a PHV range of 12.8 to 16.5 years within a single, already preselected academy.
«The 10,000-hour rule»
A journalistic popularisation. Deliberate practice explains 18 % of the variance in sport and 1%, not significant, among elite athletes.
The specific percentages of television coverage of women's sport
Not verified in full text. Verified: «little change over the past 30 years» and «dismally low».
«For every euro invested in sport, €2 of social return» (Spain)
An institutional statement to the press, not a published result. Use instead: the English ratios.
I·7 · Closing

The sentence that sums up the work

«Participation in sport is a necessary, but not sufficient condition to obtain the supposed benefits»
Fred Coalter, after eight years auditing the evidence on the social value of sport
«L'esport és una eina per educar»
Marc Gasol, explaining why his project started with a school and not with a first team

A tool. Not a miracle. What it produces depends entirely on how it is used — and that is, precisely, the responsibility and the opportunity of a development club.

Part II

The thesis, chapter by chapter

A map of the 133 pages and ~61,500 words of the full report, reconstructed from its own table of contents and from the working notes of the session in which it was produced. The full PDF is not attached in this session: to reproduce any chapter word for word, it just needs to be attached again.

II·1 · Structure

Eleven lines of research, eighteen chapters, three annexes

  1. Psychology of the young athlete Motivation, motivational climate, self-determination; why enjoyment predicts persistence and performance does not.
  2. Dropout from sport The real age (12.93), the under-14→under-16 transition, the 30% annual dropout from at least one club, and the specific case of female dropout as environmental expulsion.
  3. Talent development Early identification (the 92.8%), the relative age effect (50.3%/6.8%), early specialisation and its alternatives.
  4. Maturation and health PHV (range 12.8–16.5 years in a preselected academy), injuries and the neuromuscular warm-up (−36%), training load.
  5. Parents and coaches Parental behaviour (basketball, the worst sport analysed), presence as retention (86% vs. 58%), and behavioural training for coaches (26%→5%).
  6. Child protection LOPIVI, the European CASES project (78.1% / 69.4% / 85%), the protection officer, club culture.
  7. Sociology of the club The club as inherited, non-reproducible social capital; volunteering (~84% of the value on boards); the board as the dominant constraint.
  8. International models The SPLISS model; why Serbia and Lithuania stand out for coach education and not for budget.
  9. Management and governance Professionalisation, theory of change, the annual report as a funding instrument.
  10. Sport theory Bourdieu (capital and field), Elias (the civilising process and sport), the sport ethic of Hughes and Coakley (overconformity), and Bronfenbrenner (the ecological model of development). The framework that explains why burnout is organisational and not individual.
  11. Feminism and gender From Ann Hall and Iris Marion Young to Messner and Theberge; gender-bland sexism; the obligations of Law 39/2022. The most usable part: Young translated into technical objectives on court — «the ball is coming for me», discontinuous unity in the shot, occupying space as a season objective.

Plus the cross-cutting chapters: chapter 11 devoted to Marc Gasol (Part III), the promotion dilemma (ch. 14), the map of Catalan basketball (ch. 15), the chapter applied to CB Grup Barna, the 12-chapter editorial series (§17.12) with the table of which evidence backs each piece, and the three annexes.

II·2 · Chapter 14

The promotion dilemma

With the official FEB and ACB figures: the discontinuous cost ladder (×5 at the first national step; from Tercera to Primera FEB the fee ×36 and the bond ×19) and the two documented cases that are precisely the club's thesis:

  • Força Lleida, 2024. Promotion of the men's team to the ACB was financed, among other things, by withdrawing the women's team from Liga Femenina 2.
  • Betis / Baloncesto Sevilla, 2023. It cut €2.6m by eliminating two youth teams and the under-12 and under-10 age groups — and still ended up in insolvency proceedings in 2026 and the club disappeared.

The chapter's operational conclusion: the written promotion policy with a financial threshold and a no-cannibalisation clause (move no. 5) exists so that this cannot happen to the Barna.

II·3 · Chapter 15

The map of Catalan basketball

The national-level clubs were fully verified; the regional ones only partly, because the FCBQ website blocked automated access with a captcha. The chapter's most striking figure: there is not a single Catalan club in Primera FEB.

This line was completed later with the report «Piràmide CB Grup Barna» (13/08/2026, from basquetcatala.cat club pages): 33 federated Barna teams against the 23 youth teams of the Fundació Bàsquet Girona, the 40 of Boet Mataró (without a women's senior B) and the 24 of Santfeliuenc — with the Barna the only one of the four with two senior teams (A+B) in both genders within the same organisation.

III·2 · Synthesis

The Gasol model in five ideas, all sourced

  • The school first, the team afterwards. He founds the school in 2014; the first senior team does not arrive until 2017 (EBA). Three years of youth work before a single senior side. (FEB 2014 and 2017; Esport3)
  • Three principles, not a slogan: effort, commitment and methodology — a methodology «of our own», shared across every age group. (FEB 2014, 2017; the club's lecture at the UdG with Jesús Escosa and Marc Pena)
  • No rush, even when winning: «We are not going to rush to reach the ACB» — said weeks before winning promotion. The pace is set by solidity, not by the result. (HoyMagazine 2022)
  • The person before the sport: «Basketball is not as important as the message we can send to the younger generations to inspire them to be good people.» (El Desmarque, 2019)
  • Sport as an educational tool: «L'esport és una eina per educar» — the formulation the Barna's research chose to sum up the whole piece of work. (CBGB thesis, 2026)

It is, point by point, the same order CB Grup Barna defends in its projecte esportiu — with the measurable difference that the Barna has been doing it for sixty-one years and keeps youth and senior basketball within the same organisation.

Part VI

Diagnosis, objectives and the case

What the data proves, what it does not prove, and what can be said with it in front of a Board, the District or a sponsor without exaggerating a single figure.

VI·1 · Diagnosis

Four honest readings

What the data proves

That the club communicates above its sporting category and in line with the real size of its structure. That the brand —neighbourhood, development, women's basketball, values— generates attachment without needing an FEB senior side to tow the audience along. And that the argument is twofold: community and structure, both verifiable in public sources.

What the data does NOT prove

Neither reach, nor engagement, nor commercial value equivalent to a professional club. UEM and Prat have federation television and FEB gate receipts; Sant Adrià has a women's story of a purity a mixed account cannot replicate. Community size is one variable, not the whole picture.

The internal anomaly

32 federated teams and around 450 families sustain 7,096 followers: 222 per team. The specialists convert at twice that rate (Sant Adrià, 532; Almeda, 455). The ceiling is not set by the market or by the structure —it is the third largest in the field compared— it is set by the conversion of identity into audience.

The advantage not yet used

Those ahead are ahead by one of two routes: an FEB senior side (UEM, Prat, Cornellà) or niche purity (Sant Adrià, Almeda). The Barna has a third route nobody in the group occupies: being the medium of youth basketball —tactics, health, families, coaches— from the single account of a club that does all of it.

The sentence for the sponsorship pack

«CB Grup Barna brings together a digital community of more than 7,000 people with 32 federated teams — the third largest development structure in its comparable field and the first in the city of Barcelona by community. Ahead of five LF Endesa clubs, of every comparable club in LF Challenge, and within a single percentage point of the LF2 leaders. All of it from a single account and without a professional team.»
Verifiable in public sources · without exaggerating a single figure

To that sentence, the block of evidence in Parts I to III adds the why: it is not only that the club is big, it is that the way it develops players —enjoyment before results, no early cuts, with a complete pathway— is the one the research supports and the one a project like Marc Gasol's chose when it could have chosen any other.
