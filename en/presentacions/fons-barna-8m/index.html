Barna Fund 8M — Scholarships for Coaches

38 CB Grup Barna coaches train players every week. None holds a federation licence. For the FCBQ's statistics, they simply don't exist.

Cover letter

Barcelona, 4 March 2026

· Ref.: CB Grup Barna / 8M 2026 / FCBQ Women's Basketball Project

Dear President of the FCBQ,

CB Grup Barna is a grassroots basketball club founded in 1965 in the El Clot neighbourhood, in the Sant Martí district of Barcelona. Over 60 years of unbroken history, we have built one of the most advanced women's-basketball investment models in Catalonia: 50% of our budget goes to women's sport, when the sector standard is 35%.

Today, on the occasion of International Women's Day (8M 2026), we are writing to you and to the FCBQ's Board of Directors to set out a structural problem affecting hundreds of young Catalan women, and to propose a club-federation collaboration model that can reverse it.

We have 38 active coaches who train players every week, break stereotypes and make our model possible. None of them, however, holds a federation licence: not because they can't, but because the cost and the time required make it impossible. For the FCBQ's statistics, they simply don't exist.

We ask the Catalan Basketball Federation to recognise them and help them take the step. It is not a cost; it is an investment with a direct, measurable return in equality, in technical training and in the strength of grassroots women's basketball in Catalonia.

Yours sincerely,
CB Grup Barna

· A neighbourhood club with elite women's competition results

20 of the 35 teams are women's teams

The club has between 35 and 40 teams, with more than 450 federated members, and offers a complete pathway from pre-mini to senior with no gaps. 57% of the structure is women's: 20 women's teams out of 35.

The senior women's team competes in the Supercopa Femenina and Copa Catalunya, and outperforms the men's team competitively — investment and results go hand in hand. All of this with no home venue of its own and no specific municipal grant.

Talent exported to LF2: players trained at Barna have signed for Almeda and UE Mataró.

2 · The structural problem: the invisible gap

From player to coach

Catalan basketball has a challenge that licence numbers don't show: women join as players, but don't stay on as coaches or leaders.

Nationally, the 157,432 women's licences in 2024 represent 35.7% of the total, and women's growth is double that of men's (+19,165 women vs. +9,377 men). But there is a systematic bottleneck: women are entering basketball in growing numbers, but the mechanisms to turn them into coaches aren't keeping pace — not for lack of talent, but for lack of resources, time and recognition.

· The paradox of the FCBQ Mentoring Programme

Designed for those who already have it sorted

To take part, coaches must hold Level 0, 1, 2 or Higher qualification. The programme designed to give opportunities to coaches without formal qualifications excludes precisely the coaches who have not been able to get qualified.

· The Barna Method

The name the system was missing

The Barna Method is not a document or a programme. It's the name that was missing for something the club has been doing for 60 years, but had never articulated as an exportable system.

  • ① Inverted investment — The club puts 50% of the budget into women's basketball: a strategic bet that forces the women's model to be high quality. Investment creates expectation, expectation creates standard, standard creates role models.
  • ② The pipeline — Players don't leave the club when they stop playing: they reconvert into coaches, many leading men's teams. This cycle (escoleta → training → competition → technical staff) is what makes the club "generate women's leadership" rather than merely "have women's teams".
  • ③ The community effect — The mostly-female social-media audience is not the result of a campaign: it's the organic consequence of the two principles above.

Without a name, the Barna Method is invisible. A name turns anecdotes into a system, a system into a methodology, a methodology into an institutional and communicative asset. With the concept articulated, the club can present itself to institutions — the FCBQ, the City Council, the Generalitat, the FEB — not as "a club that does things right", but as "the creator of a replicable model".

4 · The hidden pattern: the vicious circle of women's talent

Cross-analysis of FCBQ data + drop-out studies + internal reality

Cross-analysis of FCBQ data, drop-out studies and CB Grup Barna's internal reality makes it possible to formulate the mechanism that pushes women out of basketball:

  1. 1The player stops competing at 13–16 years old.
  2. 2She finds no female role models in technical roles → doesn't consider coaching.
  3. 3If she wants to coach: she needs a qualification (Level 0: cost + time + weekends).
  4. 4Without a qualification, she cannot access the FCBQ Mentoring Programme.
  5. 5Without mentoring, she gives up on becoming a professional.
  6. 6Her base category is still left without a female role model as coach.
→ The 13-year-old girl sees no future → back to step 1

CB Grup Barna has partially broken this circle by absorbing former players as coaches. But without a licence, this pipeline is invisible to the federation system and its statistics.

What CB Grup Barna proposes: don't require a qualification to access support — give support to obtain the qualification.

· 6.1 Profile of the group

The talent the system doesn't see

Sporting background: most are, or were, players at the club. They know basketball from the inside.

Real dedication: they coach weekly, manage teams, mentor players. A function identical to that of a qualified coach.

Barriers to qualification: financial cost, availability (weekends), family and work commitments.

Direct impact: they cover the 10-to-16 age categories, where the presence of female role models matters most.

· 6.2 What the system doesn't count but exists

When the FCBQ publishes statistics on federated coaches in Catalonia, CB Grup Barna's 38 coaches don't appear. Not because they don't exist: because they don't have a licence. Catalonia shows figures for women in technical roles that are lower than reality. Sports policy gets designed with incomplete data.

6 · Proposal: Barna Fund 8M

Scholarships for coaches · three levels of action

This isn't a one-off ask: it's a club-federation collaboration model to close the qualification gap and generate a systemic change that is replicable across Catalonia.

Level 1 · immediate action

Scholarship fund for qualification

Unit cost per coach (FCBQ Level 0 Course), to be agreed jointly.

  • Priority: active coaches, with no prior qualification
  • Proposed co-financing: 30% club + 70% FCBQ (or 100% FCBQ for small clubs)
  • Retention commitment: minimum 2 seasons at the beneficiary club
  • CB Grup Barna: 38 immediate candidates
Level 2 · 6 months

Redesign of the FCBQ Mentoring Programme

Change the access criterion: from "prior qualification" to "actively coaching at a federated club".

  • Entry route based on real activity, not prior accreditation
  • Doesn't require significant financial investment
  • Just a federation policy decision
Level 3 · 1 year

"From El Clot to Catalonia" pilot programme

Turn the CB Grup Barna model into a documented, replicable pilot programme.

  • FCBQ certification as "Reference Club for Women's Technical Training"
  • Presentation to Barcelona City Council and the Consell Català de l'Esport
  • Results report, a basis for future federation policy

· Summary in three sentences

  • ① Create an FCBQ Scholarship Fund to pay for the licences of the 38 coaches already working. Immediate investment and return: 38 newly federated technical staff.
  • ② Change who can join the Mentoring Programme: from "whoever already has a licence" to "whoever is already coaching".
  • ③ Turn the CB Grup Barna model into a replicable policy for every neighbourhood club in Catalonia.

7 · Why it's urgent now

The connection to the real 8M

8M isn't the day clubs post something with a purple background. It's the annual reminder that structural inequality exists — and that it demands structural action, not symbolic gestures.

· Barcelona, capital of women's basketball

The bigger picture

Barcelona is the only major European capital without a women's team in an elite league. The city holds Catalonia's largest grassroots base but the lowest conversion rate into national categories.

Making coaches professional is a necessary condition to reverse this anomaly.

· Replicability for the FCBQ and grassroots Catalan basketball

The CB Grup Barna model is replicable at any grassroots club in Catalonia with the will to invest in women's basketball, a category pathway, and former players willing to move into coaching. Replicable club profile:

  • 150–500 federated members, 10–25 active teams
  • Significant women's representation across all categories
  • Former players connected to the club willing to coach
  • Institutional will to prioritise women's basketball

The federation has a unique role: creating the regulatory framework and the resource fund. Without the FCBQ, the CB Grup Barna model is an anecdotal exception. With the FCBQ, it can become the new standard for grassroots women's basketball in Catalonia.

9 · Conclusions and formal request to the FCBQ

They are already coaching. The system has to recognise them.

CB Grup Barna has spent 60 years, in the El Clot neighbourhood, building what many sports policies try to create in 4: an ecosystem where women don't leave basketball. But we have done it with no licence, no register, no institutional visibility.

CB Grup Barna's 38 coaches exist. They work. They train players. They break stereotypes by coaching men's teams. They reduce drop-out. But for the FCBQ's statistics, they don't exist.

· Formal request to the Catalan Basketball Federation

  1. That the FCBQ create a Scholarship Fund for qualifying active coaches with no licence, with initial funding to cover the Level 0 Sports Technician Course, with a club-federation co-financing model (proposal: 30% club / 70% FCBQ, or 100% FCBQ for small clubs).
  2. That the Coaches' Mentoring Programme amend its access criterion to include active coaches with no prior qualification, replacing the "prior licence" requirement with real, verifiable activity at a federated club.
  3. That CB Grup Barna act as a documented pilot club for the model to make it easier to replicate at other Catalan clubs, with FCBQ certification as "Reference Club for Women's Technical Training".
  4. That the FCBQ set up a transitional register of coaches in training to count the real female technical talent pool and produce statistics that reflect the true state of women's basketball in Catalonia.
