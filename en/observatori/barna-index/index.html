Observatori Barna · Research and analysis

What actually makes a good youth basketball club?

"Good values" and "good facilities" show up on almost every club's website in Barcelona. They say nothing, because they can't be compared. Here we propose ten criteria that can actually be checked, and explain how Barna itself scores against them, with real data.

Why "good values" and "good facilities" say nothing

Any family looking for a basketball club in Barcelona will find the same phrases repeated: "we develop values", "quality facilities", "a committed team". These are sincere phrases and, at the same time, useless ones: there's no way to compare them between clubs, because they say nothing about what happens when an 8-year-old loses six games in a row, how many hours a club spends training its coaches, or what happens to the player who gets the fewest minutes.

A criterion is only useful if it can be checked from the outside and if it tells one club apart from another. That's why we propose ten, concrete ones, that any family can use to evaluate any youth basketball club — not only ours.

The ten criteria of the Barna Index

  1. Continuity of methodHow many years the club has held the same development approach, and who carries it. A three-season project is not the same as sixty years with the same person still on court every week.
  2. A complete, unbroken pathA category for every age, from the youngest group to senior, with the same method. A club that only develops players up to under-14 isn't a complete development project.
  3. Real, not declared, parityThe same number of teams and the same budget for the women's and men's lines — not a token women's section next to a prioritised men's one.
  4. Who's on the benchWhat percentage of the technical staff are women, and whether the club trains its coaches or simply hires them.
  5. Child protection in writingWhether the club has a documented, accessible child-protection protocol, not just a stated intention.
  6. Try before you commitWhether the first contact is a real, no-obligation training session, or straight to sign-up and payment.
  7. Open data, not just claimsWhether the calendar, results and categories can be checked with real, up-to-date data, or have to be requested by WhatsApp every time.
  8. A real pipeline to the topWhether the club can point to specific, named players who have moved from its youth categories up to higher ones.
  9. Roots in the neighbourhoodWhether the club has been part of the neighbourhood's fabric for decades, or is a project that could be dismantled and rebuilt anywhere else without anyone noticing.
  10. The player who plays little also countsWhether the club can explain what it does for a non-starter, beyond Saturday's scoreboard.

How Barna itself scores, with real data

Not all ten criteria reduce to a single number, but most can be documented. Here is how CB Grup Barna answers today:

Continuity and a complete path. The club was founded in 1965 in the Clot, and Julio Torralba, its founder, still coaches the youngest group every week at 72. There is a category from the youngest group (age 4-8) through to senior, with more than 34 federated teams and around 450 players, with no gap left uncovered.

Real parity. The club has the same number of teams and the same budget for the women's and men's lines, and both senior teams — men's and women's — competed at the same time in the FCBQ Supercopa, the only Barcelona club with both at once.

Who's on the bench. 65.5% of the club's technical staff are women, above the sector average (35%).

Child protection. The club has a child-protection protocol and an equality plan, documented and published at /documents/, not just a statement of intent.

Try before you commit. At the youngest group and throughout September's open days, the first training session is always a no-obligation, free trial.

Open data. The calendar and results for every team sync daily with the Catalan Basketball Federation and can be checked, with the club's own forecasts and trends, at /partits/.

A real pipeline to the top. That same court at La Nau del Clot has produced Javier Torralba, now a coach at Valencia Basket Femení in the Liga Femenina Endesa; Ainhoa López, captain of Spar Girona in the same league; Roger Fornas, a forward in ACB and LEB Oro; and David Mejía, a Tercera FEB champion.

Roots in the neighbourhood. Sixty-one years in the same neighbourhood, Sant Martí, spreading training sessions and games across six nearby facilities.

Two criteria — 8 and 10 — the club can't yet document with the same precision as the rest: systematically measuring what happens to players who get the fewest minutes is, in fact, one of the topics this very Observatori will take on soon.

How to use the index to evaluate any club

This index isn't a sales pitch: it's a guide so any family can ask the right questions of any club, ours included.

  • Ask to see the child-protection protocol in writing, not just hear about it.
  • Ask whether the first training session is a genuine, no-cost trial.
  • Check whether the club can show you an up-to-date calendar and results, not a promise to "send it over".
  • Ask what percentage of the technical staff are women, and compare the answer to what another club tells you.
  • Ask for a specific name that came up through the club's own pipeline. If they can't name one, that's telling.

A genuine development club isn't afraid of these questions. If you have any doubt about how Barna answers them, ask us directly: that is, in fact, exactly what this Observatori is for.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Barna Index?

It's a set of ten comparable criteria — not statements of intent — for evaluating whether a youth basketball club is genuinely one: continuity, parity, child protection, open data, a real pipeline to higher categories, among others.

Can the Barna Index be used to choose a club other than CB Grup Barna?

Yes. The ten criteria are designed so that any family can use them with any basketball club in Barcelona, comparing concrete answers instead of generic phrases like "good values".

Which of the index's criteria does CB Grup Barna meet today?

The club documents eight of the ten with public data: sixty-one years of continuity, a complete path from the youngest group to senior, real parity in budget and teams, 65.5% female coaches, a published child-protection protocol, a free trial session, open data synced with the FCBQ, and specific names that came up through its own pipeline.
