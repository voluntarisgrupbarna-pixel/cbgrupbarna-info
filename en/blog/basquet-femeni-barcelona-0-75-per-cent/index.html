0.75%: the paradox of women's basketball in Barcelona

Barcelona has 1.6 million residents, 134 grassroots women's teams and one of the largest development bases in Europe. And a conversion rate to national categories of 0.75%. La Seu d'Urgell, with 12,000 residents, has 12.5%.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

The paradox, in one number

Of every 100 players trained in Barcelona, fewer than one reaches national-level competition. 99.25% of the female talent the city develops disappears from the system. It isn't lost at one particular stage: it just evaporates.

The first surprise is that size works against you. The bigger the city, the worse the conversion:

  • La Seu d'Urgell — 12,000 residents, ~8 grassroots teams, 1 team in LF Endesa: 12.5%.
  • Girona — 103,000 residents, ~30 teams, 2 national-level teams: 6.7%.
  • Mataró — 130,000 residents, ~25 teams, 2 teams in LF2: 4%.
  • Madrid — 3.5 million, 100+ teams, 21 national-level teams: ~4%.
  • Barcelona — 1.6 million, 134 teams, 1 fragile team in LF Challenge: 0.75%.

Why it happens

There's no single culprit. There are five causes that feed off each other.

Facility saturation. In Barcelona the sports hall is the scarce resource. When hours run short, women's senior teams are the ones left with the 9:30pm slots, and court rental can reach €110,000 a year for a club. The bill always lands on the same group.

The LF2 rung is missing. A player who grows up in the city and has outgrown regional level has nowhere to go without leaving. The ladder has a broken rung right in the middle.

Talent drain. A consequence of the above: Cornellà, Mataró and Girona take in players trained in Barcelona. The city develops talent it can't hold on to.

Male-dominated governance. Across the 17 clubs analyzed, 100% of the presidents are men. Decisions on schedules, budgets and priorities are made from a single perspective.

Shadow effect. The presence of one global club shapes the whole ecosystem, and partnerships with the city's women's clubs have never quite taken hold.

What the paradox doesn't explain

It's worth saying plainly: it isn't a problem of talent, or of desire, or of girls who don't want to play. With 134 grassroots teams, the raw material is all there. It's an architecture problem: the system is designed in a way that loses, along the way, what it produces itself.

What can be done

The levers are known and none of them is impossible: bring back the LF2 rung in the city, distribute court-time slots on equity grounds, specifically fund clubs with national-level women's representation or the potential to have it, stabilize a calendar of women's events, and support technical training for the coaches already on the court.

The club has passed this diagnosis on to Barcelona City Council's Department of Sports (February 2026) and to the Catalan Basketball Federation (March 2026), with a 2026-2030 action framework for the city.

A case that proves it can be done

CB Grup Barna is today the number-1 women's basketball club in Barcelona city by registrations — 250 female registrations, a 6.61% share among 55 federated clubs — and sits in the TOP 12 of Catalonia's 411 clubs. Put another way: one in every fifteen women's basketball players in Barcelona trains in El Clot.

Without being a big or wealthy club. With a budget of €140,000-160,000, half of it going to the women's section. The uncomfortable conclusion for the sector is that the 50% isn't asking for more money: it's asking to distribute what already exists differently. The whole model is set out on the club's women's basketball page.

Frequently asked questions

What is the conversion rate for women's basketball in Barcelona?

0.75%. Of the players trained in the city, fewer than one in a hundred reach national-level competition, far below Girona (6.7%), Mataró (4%) or La Seu d'Urgell (12.5%).

How many women's basketball teams are there in Barcelona?

Around 134 grassroots women's teams, spread across 55 federated clubs in the city.

Why do Barcelona's players have to move to other cities?

Because Barcelona is missing the LF2 rung. Once a player outgrows regional level she has nowhere to continue in the city and has to move to Cornellà, Mataró or Girona.

Which club has the most women's basketball players in Barcelona?

CB Grup Barna, with 250 female registrations and a 6.61% share of the city: one in every fifteen women's basketball players in Barcelona trains there.

Does women's basketball need more budget to improve?

Not necessarily. CB Grup Barna runs on €140,000-160,000 a year and puts 50% of it toward the women's section, when the sector norm is around 35%. The difference is in the distribution, not the volume.
