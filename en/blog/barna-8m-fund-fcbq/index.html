Barna 8M Fund: the club's proposal to the FCBQ to certify 38 coaches

CB Grup Barna has formally presented the Catalan Basketball Federation with a proposal to recognise and help certify the club's 38 coaches who, despite coaching every week, hold no federation licence.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-16

Thirty-eight coaches the system doesn't count

CB Grup Barna has 38 active female coaches this season, 65% of the club's entire technical staff. They train players, run teams, mentor, and many of them lead boys' teams. None holds a federation licence — not because they can't, but because the cost and scheduling of the course make it impossible.

For the Catalan Basketball Federation's statistics, these 38 women simply don't exist. And that isn't just a problem of individual recognition: when Catalonia designs sports policy with incomplete data, the real figures for women in technical roles fall below what's actually happening on the courts.

8 March as a reminder, not a campaign

On 4 March 2026, coinciding with International Women's Day, the club sent a formal letter to the FCBQ's president and its Board of Directors. It isn't a symbolic gesture: it's a concrete request, with figures, a co-financing model, and a club commitment to cover part of the cost.

"The future of women's basketball is already training. The system just needs to recognise it."

The proposal, in three tiers

  • Tier 1 — Certification scholarship fund. Co-financing the Level 0 Sports Coach course: proposed at 30% club, 70% FCBQ (or 100% FCBQ for small clubs), with a two-season commitment to stay. Barna has 38 candidates ready now.
  • Tier 2 — Redesign of the FCBQ Mentoring Programme. Changing the entry criteria from "prior certification" to "actively coaching at a federated club." It requires no financial investment, only a federation policy decision.
  • Tier 3 — "From El Clot to Catalonia" pilot programme. Turning Barna's model into a documented, replicable programme, with FCBQ certification as a "Reference Club for Women's Technical Development" and a presentation to Barcelona City Council and the Consell Català de l'Esport.

Why the club can make this request with authority

CB Grup Barna puts 50% of its budget into women's basketball, when the sector norm sits around 35%. 65.5% of the technical staff are women. 57% of the club's structure — 20 of its 35 teams — is women's. This isn't a one-off gesture for 8 March: it's sixty years of a model built in El Clot that had never before been put forward as a policy proposal.

What the formal request asks for exactly

  1. An FCBQ scholarship fund to certify active coaches without a licence.
  2. A change to the entry criteria for the FCBQ Mentoring Programme.
  3. For CB Grup Barna to act as a documented pilot club for the model.
  4. A transitional FCBQ register of coaches in training, to properly count Catalonia's real female technical talent.

The full document, with the letter to the Federation's president and every figure, is at Barna 8M Fund.

Frequently asked questions

What exactly does the Barna 8M Fund ask for?

An FCBQ scholarship fund to pay for the Level 0 Sports Coach course for active coaches without a licence, with a proposed 30% club / 70% FCBQ co-financing split, and a redesign of the Coach Mentoring Programme so that anyone already coaching can access it, not only those who already hold certification.

How many CB Grup Barna coaches lack a federation licence?

38, or 65% of the club's entire technical staff. They train players every week and many lead boys' teams, but they don't appear in the FCBQ's official statistics.

When was the proposal presented?

On 4 March 2026, to mark International Women's Day, in a letter addressed to the president of the Catalan Basketball Federation.
