"Shrink it and pink it": when kit design drives players away

Take a garment designed for men, shrink the size and, if anything, change the colour. The industry calls it "shrink it and pink it," and it affects everyone from the WNBA to a ten-year-old girl in El Clot.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

It isn't a question of sizing

The idea that a women's kit is just a smaller men's kit starts from an anatomical error. The differences aren't a matter of scale, they're structural: hip-to-waist ratio, shoulder width, torso length. A men's small isn't a women's small; it's an entirely different garment.

When the garment isn't designed for the body wearing it, specific things happen during a match: the shirt that keeps needing adjusting, shorts that won't stay up, underwear and shirt working against each other instead of together. None of it shows up on the scoresheet, but all of it takes something out of the player.

A documented factor in dropout

Karen Korellis Reuther, of the Harvard Graduate School of Design, has described it as a pattern that runs "from insulting to deadly," because of the injury risk tied to gear that doesn't support the way it should.

And there's a part no one puts in a federation report. A 2025 Durham University study concludes that lack of design choice in uniforms is a significant factor in female sport dropout during puberty. Ogden's 2025 study found that the recurring theme among teenage former athletes who had quit was constant worry about physical appearance because of the uniform, rather than focusing on the game.

Translated: there are players who quit basketball because of the clothing. Not because they're superficial, but because competing while feeling exposed every weekend eventually becomes unsustainable.

The detail nobody talks about

Light-coloured shorts during menstruation are a real, constant source of anxiety in competition. It's a detail that costs zero euros to solve at the design stage — dark fabric on the shorts — and that, if no one thinks about it, never gets solved. This is exactly the difference between designing for someone and designing without them in mind.

What we're doing in El Clot

Every season more than 200 girls, teenagers and women wear CB Grup Barna kit. The club is preparing the first kit collection from a grassroots club in Catalonia designed specifically for the female body, with three lines of work:

  • Patterns from scratch, not scaled down. Cut, sleeves, length and waist designed around real female bodies, with several size prototypes.
  • Functional materials and colours. Dark fabric on the shorts, breathable fabric with absorption capacity, and a waistband that doesn't need readjusting every two minutes.
  • Co-creation with the players. Prototypes, testing during training and structured feedback. The people who'll wear it decide what it looks like.

This isn't a commercial operation or a special edition. It's the logical consequence of something simple: you can't practise structural equality while players compete in clothing that wasn't designed for them. The full project is explained on the Women's Line page.

Frequently asked questions

What does "shrink it and pink it" mean?

It's the sports-industry pattern of taking a product designed for men, shrinking the size and, at best, changing the colour to sell it as women's.

Why doesn't a small size of men's kit work?

Because the differences between male and female bodies aren't a matter of scale but of structure: hip-to-waist ratio, shoulder width and torso length. A men's small isn't a women's small; it's a different garment.

Can the uniform influence dropout from sport?

Yes. Recent academic research documents that worry about physical appearance caused by the uniform is a recurring theme among teenagers who have quit sport, and that lack of design choice is a significant factor.

Why does the colour of the shorts matter?

Because light-coloured shorts during menstruation cause real anxiety about possible stains mid-competition. Dark fabric solves the problem at zero added cost if it's considered at the design stage.

What is CB Grup Barna's Women's Line?

The first kit collection from a grassroots club in Catalonia designed from scratch for the female body, with new patterns, functional materials and co-creation with the club's players.
