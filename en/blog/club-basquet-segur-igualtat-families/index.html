Eleven questions to tell if a basketball club truly looks after your daughter

Every club says it looks after its players. The difference is that some have it in writing, approved and with a named person responsible, and others just say so. These are the questions that tell them apart.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

Why you should ask

Signing up a daughter or son at a club means leaving them for several hours a week, over years, with adults you don't know, both inside and outside the facility. It's a decision usually made by looking at the schedule and the fee.

The questions below aren't meant to put anyone on the spot. A club that has this sorted out answers them in three minutes without any discomfort. One that stumbles has already told you something.

The eleven questions

  1. Do you have an approved equality plan? In writing, approved by the board, with a validity period and review date. It isn't a decorative document: it sets commitments that can be checked.
  2. Do you have a protocol against sexual harassment and harassment based on sex? It should have definitions, prevention measures, a communication channel and concrete response timelines.
  3. Who is the point person on equality matters? It has to be someone identifiable, with a name and a contact route, communicated at the start of each season.
  4. How is a situation reported and what happens next? A good protocol gives an initial evaluation timeline (72 hours is a reasonable standard), provides for precautionary measures and sets a resolution deadline.
  5. Do you check sexual-offence record certificates for the whole staff? It's legally mandatory for anyone working with minors. The answer should be an immediate yes.
  6. Do you have a designated child-protection officer? This is the role required by the LOPIVI law for organizations working with children.
  7. Is there a women's team at every age group, up to senior level? If the pathway ends at cadet, your daughter will have to leave the club right when she's most attached to it.
  8. What time does the senior women's team train? This question is an excellent detector. If the answer is "9:30 or 10pm," you already know who gets priority when court time is short.
  9. How many women coaches do you have? This can't be dressed up: either they're there or they aren't.
  10. What share of the budget goes to the women's section? Few organizations have worked this out. Whether they can answer already tells you a lot.
  11. What happens if a family can't pay the fee? Ask whether there are social scholarships and how to apply, even if you don't need them. The answer paints a picture of the club.

How we answer

For consistency, here are CB Grup Barna's answers, which you can check against reality:

  • Equality Plan 2026-2029, approved by the Board of Directors in April 2026, with five pillars, tracking indicators and an annual review.
  • Prevention and response protocol for sexual harassment and harassment based on sex, approved the same date, with a designated point person, initial evaluation within 72 hours, precautionary measures and resolution within a maximum of 15 days.
  • Child protection under the LOPIVI law, with the club's policy set out in the briefing.
  • Complete women's pathway, from the age-4 Escoleta through to senior, with no gender gap.
  • Prime evening slots (5-8pm) with priority access for women's teams.
  • 38 women coaches, 65.5% of the technical staff.
  • 50% of the budget to the women's section, with a commitment to publish the annual breakdown.
  • Social scholarships so no girl or boy is left out for financial reasons.

One last piece of advice

Ask to watch a training session before deciding anything. Fifteen minutes in the stands tells you things no document covers: how coaches talk to players when something goes wrong, who plays and who watches, and whether those on the court seem happy to be there.

Frequently asked questions

Does a basketball club have to have an equality plan?

Not every organization is required to by size, but having one approved by the board, with indicators and an annual review, is the clearest sign the commitment is real and not just a statement.

What is the LOPIVI law and how does it affect a sports club?

It's Spain's comprehensive law protecting children and adolescents against violence. It requires organizations working with minors to have protection measures and a designated point person, and to check staff members' sexual-offence record certificates.

What should an anti-harassment protocol include?

Clear definitions, prevention measures, an identifiable point person, a confidential communication channel, evaluation and resolution timelines, precautionary measures, and a guarantee against retaliation.

Why does the senior women's training time matter?

Because when court time is scarce, it reveals who gets priority. A 9:30-11pm slot makes it impossible for 16- or 17-year-old players to train there during the school term.

Does CB Grup Barna offer scholarships for families?

Yes. The club maintains social scholarships so no girl or boy is left out for financial reasons. You can ask about them by writing to the club, with no commitment.
