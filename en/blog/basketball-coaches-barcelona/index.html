Women's basketball

Basketball coaches: why it matters who's on the bench

35.7% of basketball licences in Spain belong to women. Among Barcelona club chairs, 0%. Between those two extremes there's a ladder that loses people at every rung — always the same people.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

The ladder of recognition

The gender gap in sport isn't well measured by counting how many women play. It's measured by looking at how many move from one rung to the next. Here are basketball's numbers:

  • Licensed players: 35.7%. A healthy base.
  • Coaches in training (levels 0-3): 24.1%. A third is lost along the way.
  • Advanced Coaching Course: 9%. Here almost two out of three drop off.
  • Chairs of regional federations: 5%. Token.
  • Chairs of Barcelona clubs: 0%. Of the 17 clubs analysed, none.

When the drop between two consecutive rungs exceeds 40%, you're no longer looking at a matter of vocation: you're looking at a barrier. And there are three in a row.

An empty bench isn't neutral

A girl who spends ten years at a club without ever seeing a woman leading a team learns something no one ever told her outright. No one needs to say coaching isn't for her: it's enough that she never sees one do it.

There's one detail in this story that's hard to swallow and that matters: when players see a female coach for the first time, they tend to rate her as less competent than a male coach. But sustained exposure flips the bias entirely — players who have had female coaches rate them as equally competent as men. In other words: the fix isn't a talk, it's normality repeated for years.

The moment everything changes: a woman coaching a boys' team

Inside a club, the truly transformative step isn't a woman coaching the girls' team. It's her coaching the boys' team. When that happens, three things occur at once that no equality programme can manufacture:

  • Girls see that leading is a normal role for a woman.
  • Boys grow up with a female sporting authority figure, and it seems completely normal to them because it's the only thing they've known.
  • Families see first-hand that a woman is competent to coach their son. That moves more than any campaign.

The certification paradox

CB Grup Barna has 38 active female coaches. They make up 65.5% of the technical staff and coach 40 teams every week. None of them holds a federation licence — not for lack of ability, but because of the cost of the course and the schedule it demands, which clashes with studying or working.

This has a consequence that reaches far beyond the club: for official statistics, these 38 women don't exist. Multiply that across every club in Catalonia in the same situation and you get figures for women in technical roles that sit well below reality. And sports policy gets designed around those figures.

On top of that, support programmes usually require certification to access them. In other words: you need to already have what the programme is supposed to help you get.

The Barna 8M Fund

On 4 March 2026 the club formally presented the Catalan Basketball Federation with a proposal to break this cycle: a scholarship fund covering the Level 0 Sports Coach course, co-financed by the club and the federation, with a two-season commitment to stay. The club has 38 candidates ready to go.

The second part of the proposal costs nothing: changing the entry criteria for the mentoring programme from "having prior certification" to "actively coaching at a federated club." An entry route based on real activity, not paperwork.

The full formal request, with the letter to the FCBQ president and the proposal's three tiers, is at Barna 8M Fund.

Why this should matter to a family

Because the number of female coaches a club has is one of the most honest indicators there is. It can't be improvised for a visit or dressed up in a brochure: they're either there or they aren't, and you have them in front of you every Tuesday. If you're club-shopping, count them.

Frequently asked questions

How many female coaches does CB Grup Barna have?

38 active female coaches, making up 65.5% of the club's technical staff and coaching 40 teams every week.

What percentage of coaches are women in basketball overall?

Women make up 35.7% of player licences, 24.1% of staff in technical training, and only 9% of the Advanced Coaching Course. Among the chairs of the Barcelona clubs analysed, 0%.

Do you need certification to coach at a grassroots club?

Federation certification is the formal route, but many active female coaches don't have it because of the course's cost and schedule. The Barna 8M Fund proposes funding it with a commitment to stay at the club.

What is the Barna 8M Fund?

A CB Grup Barna proposal presented to the Catalan Basketball Federation on 4 March 2026 to fund certification for active female coaches and change the entry criteria for mentoring programmes.

Why does it matter for a woman to coach a boys' team?

Because it normalises female sporting authority for girls, boys and families all at once. It's sustained exposure, not communication, that changes perception.
