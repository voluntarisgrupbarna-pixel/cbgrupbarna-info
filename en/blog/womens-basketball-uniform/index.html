“Shrink it and pink it”: when the kit drives players away

Take a garment designed for men, shrink the size and, at best, change the colour. The industry calls it “shrink it and pink it,” and it affects everyone from the WNBA to a ten-year-old girl in El Clot.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

It isn't a matter of sizing

The idea that a women's kit is just a smaller men's kit starts from an anatomical error. The differences aren't a matter of scale, they're structural: hip-to-waist ratio, shoulder width, torso length. A men's S isn't a women's S; it's a different garment altogether.

When a garment isn't built for the body wearing it, specific things happen during a game: the jersey that keeps needing adjusting, the shorts that won't stay at the waist, underwear and jersey working against each other instead of together. None of this shows up in the box score, but it all adds up.

A documented dropout factor

Karen Korellis Reuther, of the Harvard Graduate School of Design, has described it as a pattern that "ranges from insulting to deadly," given the injury risk tied to gear that doesn't support the way it should.

And there's a part no federation report mentions. A 2025 Durham University study concludes that the lack of design options in uniforms is a significant factor in female sports dropout during puberty. Ogden's 2025 study found that the recurring theme among teenage former athletes who had quit was constant self-consciousness about their appearance caused by the uniform, instead of focusing on the game.

Translated: some players quit basketball because of the clothing. Not because they're superficial, but because competing while feeling exposed every weekend eventually becomes unsustainable.

The detail no one talks about

Light-coloured shorts during a period are a real, constant source of anxiety mid-competition. It's a detail that costs nothing to fix at the design stage — dark fabric on the shorts — and that, if no one thinks about it, never gets fixed. That's exactly the difference between designing for someone and designing without them in mind.

What we're doing in El Clot

Every season, more than 200 girls, teenagers and women wear CB Grup Barna's kit. The club is preparing the first grassroots club collection in Catalonia designed from scratch for the female body, with three lines of work:

  • Patterns built from zero, not shrunk. Cut, sleeves, length and waist designed around real female bodies, with several size prototypes.
  • Functional materials and colours. Dark fabric on the shorts, breathable fabric with absorption capacity, and a waistband that doesn't need readjusting every two minutes.
  • Co-creation with the players. Prototypes, testing during training and structured feedback. The people who'll wear it decide what it looks like.

This isn't a commercial move or a limited edition. It's the logical consequence of something simple: you can't practise structural equality while players compete in clothing that wasn't designed for them. The full project is explained on the Women's Line page.

Frequently asked questions

What does “shrink it and pink it” mean?

It's the sportswear industry pattern of taking a product designed for men, shrinking the size and, at best, changing the colour to sell it as women's.

Why doesn't a small men's kit work for women?

Because the differences between male and female bodies aren't a matter of scale but structural: hip-to-waist ratio, shoulder width and torso length. A men's S isn't equivalent to a women's S.

Can the uniform affect sports dropout?

Yes. Recent academic research documents that self-consciousness about appearance caused by the uniform is a recurring theme among teens who have quit sport, and that the lack of design options is a significant factor.

Why does the colour of the shorts matter?

Because light-coloured shorts during a period cause real anxiety about visible stains mid-competition. Dark fabric solves the problem at no extra cost if it's considered at the design stage.

What is CB Grup Barna's Women's Line?

The first kit collection by a grassroots club in Catalonia designed from scratch for the female body, with new patterns, functional materials and co-creation with the club's players.
