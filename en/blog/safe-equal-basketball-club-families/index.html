Eleven questions to know if a basketball club truly cares for your daughter

Every club says it takes care of its players. The difference is that some have it in writing, approved, with a named person responsible — and others just say it. These are the questions that tell them apart.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

Why it's worth asking

Signing up a daughter or son at a club means leaving them for several hours a week, for years, with adults you don't know, inside and outside the facility. It's a decision usually made by looking at the schedule and the fee.

The questions below aren't meant to put anyone on the spot. A club that has this sorted answers them in three minutes without discomfort. One that stumbles has already told you something.

The eleven questions

  1. Do you have an approved equality plan? In writing, approved by the board, with a validity period and a review date. It isn't a decorative document: it sets commitments that can be checked.
  2. Do you have a protocol against sexual and gender-based harassment? It should include clear definitions, prevention measures, a communication channel and concrete response times.
  3. Who is the point person for equality matters? It should be someone identifiable, with a name and a way to reach them, announced at the start of every season.
  4. How is a situation reported, and what happens next? A good protocol sets an initial assessment window (72 hours is a reasonable standard), allows for precautionary measures, and sets a resolution deadline.
  5. Do you check the whole staff's sexual offence certificates? It's a legal requirement for anyone working with minors. The answer should be an immediate yes.
  6. Do you have a child protection officer? This is the role required by law for entities working with children.
  7. Is there a women's team at every category, up to senior? If the pathway stops at cadet, your daughter will have to leave the club right when she's most attached to it.
  8. What time does the senior women's team train? This question is a great detector. If the answer is "9:30 or 10pm," you already know who gets priority when courts are scarce.
  9. How many female coaches do you have? It can't be dressed up: either they're there or they aren't.
  10. What share of the budget goes to the women's section? Few clubs have this worked out. Whether they can answer already says a lot.
  11. What happens if a family can't afford the fee? Ask whether there are social grants and how to apply, even if you don't need them. The answer paints a picture of the club.

How we answer these ourselves

For consistency, here are CB Grup Barna's own answers, which you can verify:

  • 2026-2029 Equality Plan, approved by the Board of Directors in April 2026, with five action lines, tracking indicators and an annual review.
  • Prevention and response protocol for sexual and gender-based harassment, approved on the same date, with a designated point person, an initial assessment within 72 hours, precautionary measures and resolution within a maximum of 15 days.
  • Child protection under the LOPIVI, with the club's policy set out in the briefing.
  • A full women's pathway, from the 4-year-old Escoleta to senior level, with no gender break at all.
  • Priority afternoon slots (5pm-8pm) for women's teams.
  • 38 female coaches, 65.5% of the technical staff.
  • 50% of the budget going to the women's section, with a commitment to publish the breakdown every year.
  • Social grants so no child is left out for financial reasons.

One last piece of advice

Ask to watch a training session before deciding anything. Fifteen minutes in the stands reveal things no document captures: how players are spoken to when something goes wrong, who plays and who watches, and whether the kids out there look happy to be there.

Frequently asked questions

Does a basketball club need to have an equality plan?

Not every entity is legally required to by size, but having one approved by the board, with indicators and an annual review, is the clearest sign the commitment is real rather than just declarative.

What is the LOPIVI and how does it affect a sports club?

It's Spain's law on the comprehensive protection of children and adolescents against violence. It requires organisations working with minors to have protection measures and a designated officer, and to check staff members' sexual offence certificates.

What should an anti-harassment protocol contain?

Clear definitions, prevention measures, an identifiable point person, a confidential communication channel, assessment and resolution deadlines, precautionary measures and a guarantee against retaliation.

Why does the senior women's training time matter?

Because when courts are scarce, it reveals who gets priority. A 9:30-11pm slot makes it impossible for 16 or 17-year-old players to train there during the school term.

Does CB Grup Barna offer grants to families?

Yes. The club maintains social grants so no child is left out for financial reasons. They can be enquired about by writing to the club, with no commitment.
