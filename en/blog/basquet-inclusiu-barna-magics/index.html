Inclusive basketball: how an adapted-sport team works

The Barna Màgics aren't a corporate-social-responsibility programme. They're a team of the club: with their own calendar, weekly training sessions, coaching staff and the same shirt as everyone else.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-14

What the Barna Màgics are

It's CB Grup Barna's basketball team for people with intellectual disabilities. It trains on the same court as the rest of the teams, shares the sports hall and changing rooms, competes on its own calendar and appears on the same schedule as the cadet or senior teams. The project runs in partnership with the Fundació Mullor and ACELL, the Catalan federation for sport for people with intellectual disabilities.

The important distinction is this: it isn't a side activity the club runs on top of everything else. It's a team within the structure, on equal footing with the others.

Why the difference matters

An inclusion programme that lives as an add-on ends up depending on who's running it and whether there's budget for it that year. A team built into the club's structure has the same guarantees as any other: an assigned court, coaching staff, a calendar and a place in communications.

It also changes what happens at the facility. When an adapted-sport team trains alongside the youth teams, the club's children grow up seeing it as the most normal thing in the world, because for them it is. No awareness talk is needed: sharing a sports hall for years does the job.

What a training session looks like

It's much closer to any other team's session than people imagine. Warm-up, technical work, game situations and a scrimmage. They work on shooting mechanics, defensive positioning and the rules. What changes is the pace of progression and how things are explained, not the standards or the respect.

Competition happens within ACELL's circuit and, in the context of adapted sport, at Special Olympics-type gatherings. During the 2025-26 season the club has been a leading participant in the Catalan Basketball Federation's +BÀSQUET programme across the territory.

Who can take part

People with intellectual disabilities who want to play basketball, regardless of prior level. No experience is needed: some arrive never having touched a ball. As with the rest of the teams, the first day is a no-commitment trial.

If you're interested for someone in your family, write to us and we'll explain schedules and how registration works: you can do so via the club's WhatsApp or by leaving your details on the form.

The context: a club that does it all at once

Adapted sport is usually the first thing to go when a club is stretched thin. In El Clot it coexists with 1:1 team parity, with 50% of the budget going to women's basketball and a complete pathway from age 4. Not because the club is big —it isn't— but because it's how you understand what a club is for in a neighborhood.

It's worth saying it as it is: a team like the Màgics doesn't deliver flashy sporting results or headlines. It delivers something else, and anyone who's spent an afternoon in the stands at La Nau del Clot during one of their games knows exactly what.

Frequently asked questions

What are the Barna Màgics?

CB Grup Barna's basketball team for people with intellectual disabilities, built into the club's structure on equal footing with the rest of the teams, in partnership with the Fundació Mullor and ACELL.

Do you need previous experience to play?

No. Some players arrive never having played before. As with the rest of the club's teams, the first day is a no-commitment trial.

Where do they train and compete?

They train at La Nau del Clot, the same facility as the rest of the club's teams, in Barcelona's Sant Martí District, and compete within ACELL's adapted-sport circuit.

What is the FCBQ's +BÀSQUET programme?

A Catalan Basketball Federation programme to spread basketball practice across the territory. CB Grup Barna has been a leading participant during the 2025-26 season.

How can someone sign up for the team?

By writing to the club's WhatsApp (+34 698 425 153) or leaving contact details on the website form. The club explains schedules, how registration works, and arranges a first trial session.

Want information about the club?

Leave us your name and contact details and we'll get back to you with information about the Escoleta, the camp or whichever team fits.
