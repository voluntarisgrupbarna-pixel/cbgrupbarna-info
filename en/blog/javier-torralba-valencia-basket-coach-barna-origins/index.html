Javier Torralba, Valencia Basket's new women's head coach: from CB Grup Barna origins to Spain's top league

He started playing basketball in the Clot at age four. Sixty-one years after the club's founding, Javier Torralba is now head coach of Valencia Basket's women's team, one of European women's basketball's biggest projects. In a lengthy interview with womenvalenciabasket.com (2 September 2026), he talks about his roots at the Barna, his father, and his first week in Valencia.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-09-03

Who is Javier Torralba

Javier Torralba is a basketball coach trained on the court in the Clot. He grew up playing at CB Grup Barna, where his father — still active, in his 70s — has also coached his whole life. After a career as a youth-development coach and with Spain's national youth selections, this 2026/27 season he has taken charge of the women's first team of Valencia Basket, four-time defending league champions, in the Liga Femenina Endesa.

"My essence": the bond with the Barna

During the interview, Torralba was shown a photograph from his childhood at CB Grup Barna. He says he started playing there at four, that it's the club where his father still coaches and where his own children play today, and calls it "my essence". Looking back on those years, he says what stays with him most is gratitude to the club that gave him everything, along with values such as consistency, generosity and a sense of family — things, he says, he carries with him every day.

That child playing in the Clot wasn't yet dreaming of coaching: he just wanted to enjoy himself with a ball. As a teenager, he explains, basketball gave him structure and commitment, and helped him find a path.

His father, an example on and off the court

One of the interview's most emotional moments came when he watched a video of his father sending him a message of support. Torralba explains that his father's influence has mattered more in his life than in his career, and that what has shaped him most is his father's human side: how he went out of his way for the players he coached, some of whom became practically part of the family. In his 70s, his father is still coaching with a passion Torralba describes as a model to follow.

The first week at Valencia Basket

Torralba joined the orange-and-black club on the recommendation of, among others, Rubén Burgos, whom he met through Spain's youth national teams. He describes the first week of pre-season as very positive, even though it started with only three first-team players available while the rest of the squad joined later, many of them on international duty. On settling in at the club, he highlights that the support goes beyond basketball itself: the whole environment at Valencia Basket, he says, is built so the coaching staff can focus purely on their objectives.

The identity he wants to build

Asked about the identity he wants for the new-look Valencia Basket, after four straight league titles won by the club, Torralba sums up his ambition in the line that gives the original interview its headline: he wants "people to feel proud" of the team. He describes his vision of the game as a dynamic, brave team where many players get room to show what they can do.

He also reflected on the moment women's basketball is living through, with the rise of the NCAA and the expansion of the WNBA: he believes Spain must keep proving that the players who stay develop top-level careers, and that European women's basketball can remain at a very high level even as the international landscape shifts.

Why it matters to us at the Barna

Javier Torralba isn't the only player trained in the Clot to reach the top level: there's also Ainhoa López, captain of Spar Girona in the Liga Femenina Endesa, Roger Fornas, a centre in the ACB and LEB Oro, and other players later developed at FC Barcelona. A former academy player now coaching a top-level women's first team confirms something we tell families joining the Escoleta often: what you learn at a neighbourhood club — consistency, generosity, adaptability — is exactly what it takes to reach the top, wherever you end up playing or coaching afterwards.
