How to choose a basketball school in Barcelona

Barcelona has dozens of clubs and they all say the same thing on their website. These are the seven points that truly set one basketball school apart from another.

CB Grup Barna · 2026-08-05

1. How many coaches are there per group

It's the first thing you should ask and the thing people ask least. A group of twenty five-year-olds with a single coach isn't a basketball school: it's a daycare with balls. With two adults per group, the session changes completely.

2. Whether groups are split by age or by convenience

A four-year-old and a seven-year-old can't train together: they don't have the same body or the same attention span. Clubs that take this seriously split the introductory age range into three groups. Those that don't lump everyone together and call it "level-based work".

3. The distance from home

It sounds mundane and it's decisive. An activity that means crossing half the city twice a week gets dropped by February. A neighborhood club holds up for years, because the child gets there alone as they grow up, and because their teammates are the same kids from school and the park.

4. What happens when they turn eight

This is the question that separates a school from an academy. If the club has federated teams in every category, the child moves from the escoleta to premini without changing place or friends. If it doesn't, at age eight they'll need to find a new club and start from scratch.

5. Whether there's a real women's programme

"We have a girls' team" isn't enough. Check how many women's teams there are, in which categories, and whether they reach senior level. A club with girls only at the youngest ages is a club where girls end up leaving.

6. The fee, and what it includes

Ask for the full annual price: fee, kit, federation registration and travel. The difference between clubs is usually smaller than it looks once you compare like for like.

7. Whether they let you try it out

Any club that trusts what it does will let you go along for a day and do a full session. If they make excuses, you already have your answer.

And the Barna Escoleta?

At CB Grup Barna, in the El Clot neighborhood, the basketball school is for children aged 4 to 8, with mixed teams, three age groups and get-togethers with other clubs from Barcelona and the province. On turning eight, children move up into the club's own federated teams, of which there are more than thirty-four. It's run by Julio Torralba (646 205 526), and the first session is a no-commitment trial.

Players who went through this court have gone on to reach the ACB, the Liga Femenina Endesa and FC Barcelona. That's not the reason to sign up a five-year-old, but it says something about the place's continuity.

Frequently asked questions

What should you look for in a basketball school?

The points that really make a difference are: number of coaches per group, whether groups are split by age, distance from home, whether the club has federated teams in every category to continue in, whether it has a real women's programme, the full annual cost, and whether they allow a trial session.

How much does a basketball school cost in Barcelona?

It varies by club. Always ask for the full annual cost —fees, kit, federation registration and travel— so the comparison between clubs is a fair one.

What basketball school is there in El Clot or Sant Martí?

CB Grup Barna runs its Escoleta in the El Clot neighborhood, Sant Martí District, for children aged 4 to 8, with continuity into the club's federated teams from age 8.

Can you try before signing up?

Yes, and it's highly recommended. At CB Grup Barna the first session is a no-commitment trial; you can book a day with Julio Torralba at 646 205 526.
