History of CB Grup Barna

Sixty years in the El Clot neighbourhood: where the name comes from, where the colours come from, and who has come out of this youth system since 1965.

1965 · A section within a multi-sport club

The Club Bàsquet Grup Barna was born in 1965 as the basketball section of the Centre Parroquial de Sant Martí del Clot. It didn't start out as a basketball club: it started as a multi-sport organisation open to all the young people of the neighbourhood, at a time when organised sport was almost exclusive to religious and elite schools, the only ones with access to good facilities.

In neighbourhoods that lacked that privilege, sport arrived through personal initiative. In El Clot it arrived by making use of the simple facilities of the old Rodó. In its early years the club also had a table tennis section —which went on to win three Barcelona Championships (1968, 1969, 1970) and a Spanish Championship— until it was eventually discontinued and Grup Barna became what it is today: an organisation dedicated exclusively to basketball.

What the club has always claimed as most important is not its sporting record, brilliant as it may be, but something else: a place where generations of boys and girls from El Clot feel at home, learn to play, and form friendships that in many cases last a lifetime. This is still, today, the purpose of a non-profit sports organisation.

Where the name "Grup Barna" comes from

The version that has survived to this day, reconstructed from the collective memory of the club's elders, begins with Lluís Bouyssi, Joan Manuel Serrat and a few friends who returned to Barcelona from Tarragona and decided to form a "Grup de Barcelona d'antics estudiants de la Universitat Laboral de Tarragona" (Barcelona Group of former students of the Tarragona Labour University). The name was too long for everyday use, and "Barcelona" was shortened to "Barna". That's how the name Grup Barna was born.

Where the colours come from

The story behind the colours is less clear, but two facts have been passed down from generation to generation. This group of friends played their first matches wearing the red shirt of the Labour University team —with numbers made of plastic cut-outs sewn on by hand— and that red was kept through the club's first season once it was founded.

Black, on the other hand, was Lluís Bouyssi's favourite colour, which he always wore from head to toe. The story that has stuck is that Bouyssi ended up combining his preferred colour with the red already in use, to keep continuity with the group's origin. That's how the black and red combination the club has worn ever since was born: the first short-sleeved shirt had a black body and red sleeves, precisely so as not to lose the colour of the original team.

That's how it was, or how it's believed to have been: the origin of a name and colours that are still the club's own, sixty years on.

The youth system

From the collective effort of players, coaches and board members alike, names have emerged that left their mark on Catalan basketball. In the 1970s, 80s and 90s, among others, Germán González (Joventut de Badalona, Manresa, Caixa de Ronda, Caixa Canàries), Eduardo González (Premià, Canet, Vic) and Jordi Grau, a point guard capped internationally with the Spanish Youth National Team (Licor 43, Caixa de Ronda).

El Clot's youth system hasn't stopped producing talent. From the same court have come, in more recent generations, Iván García and Fabian Téllez; and today, Javier Torralba (coach of Valencia Basket Femení in Lliga Femenina Endesa), Ainhoa López (captain of Spar Girona in the same league), Roger Fornas (ACB and LEB Or) and David Mejía (Tercera FEB champion), as well as several players later formed in FC Barcelona's youth categories.

The club, today

Sixty years after its founding, Grup Barna keeps the same location in the El Clot neighbourhood and the same identity: a non-profit neighbourhood club with 40 federated teams and more than 450 players. In 2025 the club celebrated its 60th anniversary with a calendar of events that placed women's basketball at the centre of the celebration.

The club's current model —equal investment, a pipeline of women's leadership, and the role of the Escoleta as an entry point— is documented on the women's basketball page and in the club's official data.
